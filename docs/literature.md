The calibration set contains **twelve TOPLAS research articles, five foundational/influential works, and five adjacent-venue papers**. The categories overlap in subject but not in the quota below. ## E. Precise retained contribution and non-claims

The closest bodies of work jointly cover product constructions, shared choice identities, finite-state reachability, canonical decision representations, and checker-oriented evidence. The article therefore does **not** claim invention of any of those ingredients or a general scheduler-equivalence theory.

Within the declared explicit acyclic controller model, the retained contribution is the following combined contract:

1. a path-exact quotient that exposes named immutable inputs lazily and forgets a binding exactly when neither controller can read it again;
2. a positive closed-safe-set certificate and a negative ranked safe-prefix certificate whose acceptance proves the globally least separating budget, not only inequivalence;
3. complexity boundaries showing ordinary equivalence coNP-complete, exact least budget DP-complete, and exact semantic key irrelevance coNP-complete; and
4. selector families showing exponential size is unavoidable for the specified **explicit-record** positive and minimality certificates, while leaving symbolic formats open.

No consulted source states this four-part finite contract. This is a theorem-level non-subsumption statement about the inspected results, not a priority claim over all literature. The paper remains an internal draft until human authors validate the proofs, novelty search, accountability, and current venue rules.

## F. Additional shortest-distinction and witness evidence

Two newer primary records sharpen the comparison without changing the fixed 12+5+5 quota. Różowski's ICALP 2024 paper gives a sound and complete quantitative axiomatization of shortest-distinguishing-word distance for regular languages. Its distance is indexed by input-word length, not by the instruction budget of two controllers running over one immutable world. Turkenburg et al.'s CSL 2026 paper gives an inductive derivation system for lower bounds on behavioural distances of labelled Markov chains, with soundness and approximate completeness and a correspondence to quantitative modal formulas. Its probabilistic metric/fixed-point setting is different from exact cumulative-result equality and does not supply a universal safe prefix proving that no shared world separates earlier. The official DROPS records and exported BibTeX identify the versions cited in the manuscript.
